# Keep to your own right: in every extraction run's words, not a kept rule

Issue #69 (RF-14). Sources keyed as in `../sources.md`.

Not one of RF-11's kept rules, but graded because it is the round's clearest pattern and the side
the supervisor named ("keep left"):

- **Each of RF-11's three runs wrote a keep-right rule**; none was kept (their `predict` lists
  disagreed) and none beat "always right" on the first plan's side (RF-11, section 4).
- **The pairs mostly passed right to right:** 47 of 76 resolved sessions in RF-11's main table; 67 of
  116 resolved in the 216 sessions of RF-10's re-execution and RF-30 outside the exact-repeat set. In
  groups too: groups passed right to right in 20 of 24 (RF-34).
- **But the agents say keep left:** 66 of 384 debriefs say they kept left, citing Singapore custom;
  those agents passed left 33 times and right 33 times (RF-11, section 5).
- RF-10: being told one is visiting from the United States makes a first plan turn right, OR 2.37
  [1.42, 3.95], uncorrected.

## Evidence

- **A right preference is measured where traffic keeps right:** France ([Moussaid2009], section 4.2:
  "a slight preference for the right-hand side", amplified when both move); Beijing, 68.75% of 960
  samples detoured right ([CircleAntipode2019], Table 1, a preprint); Korea, 73.7% preferred the right
  ([Jung2013], abstract).
- **Left where it is the convention:** Japan ([Echeverria2026]).
- **Singapore:** guidance says keep left ([MOT2024]); no study of what people do was found.
- The side is a convention, not a fixed human trait, and not simply car traffic's side
  ([Moussaid2009], section 5).

## Grade

- **As a rule for Singapore: No evidence.** Nothing measured supports keeping right in Singapore, and
  the official guidance says the opposite. It would be **Contradicted** if a study showed Singapore
  pedestrians keep left; none was found.
- **As a description of the model: a finding.** The agents' behaviour matches the right-hand
  convention of the countries where most measured pedestrian data comes from, while their words match
  Singapore's guidance. That is evidence of what the model learned, reported as the model's bias, not
  as a rule about people.

## Decision

Not a staged rule; nothing to promote or drop. For the next extraction (`extract_v2`, RF-11's open
question 2): a pair-level target (the pair's passing side) would let this be scored as what it is.
