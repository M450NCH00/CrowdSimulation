## 1. Research question

What interactional patterns advanced EFL learners use in disagreement episodes that do,
and do not, bring a disagreement to a close: the study sets out to find "the
interactional patterns advanced EFL participants use in disagreement episodes", in
post-completion sequences "that do and do not bring disagreements to a close"
(Abstract).

## 2. The categories / scheme

Two **closure acts** and two **non-resolution conditions**, all named in the abstract.
Definitions beyond the wording quoted here are `[not accessible]`.

**Acts that close a disagreement sequence** (Abstract):

| Paper's label | Paper's wording | Locator |
|---|---|---|
| **relinquishing a position** | "relinquishing a position by accepting the position maintained by another" | Abstract |
| **acknowledging the repair** | "acknowledging the repair performed by another party" | Abstract |

**Conditions under which disagreements are NOT resolved** (Abstract) — the part the
project specifically needs, since LLM agents may also fail to resolve:

| Paper's label | Paper's wording | Locator |
|---|---|---|
| failure to accept plural positions | disagreements are not resolved "when the partners fail to accept the possibility of two or more positions" | Abstract |
| less-knowledgeable speaker oriented to disagreement | "when the less knowledgeable speaker is oriented to disagreement" | Abstract |

The environment in which these occur is also named: **anticipatory completions**, i.e.
"[a] potential place in which the second speaker expresses disagreement with the first
speaker", analysed in **post-completion sequences** (Abstract).

## 3. Empirical basis

All from the Abstract:
- **50 hours** of interaction;
- **20 video-recorded** English discussion task sessions;
- collected over **10 weeks**;
- **15 Farsi-speaking advanced EFL students**, working in **groups of three**.

**Setting and conflict type:** classroom group work on discussion tasks — peer
interaction, but within an instructional setting, and the non-resolution finding involves
an **epistemic asymmetry** ("the less knowledgeable speaker"). The paper notes resolving
disagreements "can pose an interactional challenge for students in classroom contexts"
(Abstract).

**Derived from this data or imported?** The study "draw[s] on Conversation Analysis to
describe" the data (Abstract), and the categories are presented as results rather than as
an imported scheme — but whether any prior scheme was applied is `[not accessible]`.

## 6. Operationalisation — how a category gets assigned

**Partially accessible**, and only at the level of the abstract's own wording:
- **relinquishing a position** — assigned where one party accepts the position the other
  has maintained (Abstract);
- **acknowledging the repair** — assigned where closure follows one party's repair being
  acknowledged by the other (Abstract);
- **non-resolution** — assigned where partners do not accept that two or more positions
  can stand, or where the less knowledgeable speaker is the one oriented to disagreement
  (Abstract).

**What textual evidence marks each in a transcript — the turn shapes, tokens or sequential
positions a coder would look for — is in the full text and is `[not accessible]`.**

## 8. Relevance to this FYP

- **It is the only source in the selected six that names conditions for NON-resolution**
  (Abstract). That matters because two LLM agents may deadlock, and a scheme with no
  category for failure cannot describe that outcome. [inference: the relevance of
  non-resolution to deadlock is my judgement; the paper is about classroom talk.]
- **"Relinquishing a position" is a candidate label for one agent conceding** (Abstract).
  Whether it is the same construct as "submission" in paper 05 or "obliging" in paper 04
  is **deliberately not decided here** — the issue forbids that mapping, and it needs
  supervisor sign-off.
- **Transfer is limited in ways worth stating**: the speakers are language learners doing
  an instructional task in an L2, so proficiency and classroom authority shape the talk;
  and one non-resolution condition depends on *relative knowledge* between speakers, which
  has no obvious analogue for two symmetric LLM agents. [inference]
