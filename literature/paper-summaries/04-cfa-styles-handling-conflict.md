## 1. Research question

Whether the five ROCI-II subscales show construct validity, and whether the five-factor
structure holds across groups: "The objective of this study was to assess the construct
validity of the five subscales of the Rahim Organizational Conflict Inventory-II"
(§Introduction, p. 122); restated in the Discussion as investigating "the construct
validity of the five ROCI-II subscales and their factor invariance across groups"
(§Discussion, p. 130).

## 2. The categories / scheme

Five styles, formed by crossing **concern for self** with **concern for others**
(§"Theory of Conflict Styles", pp. 122–123, Figure 1). Definitions as the paper gives
them:

| Paper's label | Paper's definition (short quotes) | Locator |
|---|---|---|
| **Integrating (IN)** | "high concern for self as well as the other party"; "concerned with collaboration between parties (i.e., openness, exchange of information, and examination of differences)" | p. 123 |
| **Obliging (OB)** | "low concern for self and high concern for the other party"; the person "attempts to play down the differences and emphasizes commonalities" | p. 123 |
| **Dominating (DO)** | "high concern for self and low concern for the other party"; "identified with a win-lose orientation or with forcing behavior" | p. 123 |
| **Avoiding (AV)** | "low concern for self as well as for the other party"; "associated with withdrawal, passing-the-buck, sidestepping" | p. 123 |
| **Compromising (CO)** | "moderate concern for self as well as the other party"; "associated with give-and-take or sharing" | p. 123 |

**Competing taxonomies the same paper records** (p. 123) — listed because they are part of
what this paper says, not merged with the five above:
- two styles: cooperation and competition (Deutsch);
- three styles: nonconfrontation, solution-orientation, control (Putnam & Wilson, 1982);
- four styles: yielding, problem solving, inaction, contending (Pruitt, 1983);
- Hocker and Wilmot's three-type clustering: "(1) avoidance, (2) competitive
  (distributive) and (3) collaborative (integrative)" (quoted at p. 123);
- Weider-Hatfield's (1988) conclusion that "individuals might select among three, not
  five, distinct conflict styles" (quoted at p. 123, citing her p. 364).

## 3. Empirical basis

**Five samples, 2,076 respondents in total** (Abstract, p. 122):
- 484 full-time employed management students;
- 550 public administrators;
- 214 university administrators;
- 250 bank managers and employees **in Bangladesh**;
- 578 managers and employees.

**Setting and conflict type:** organisational conflict, measured separately by referent
role — the instrument "contains Forms A, B, and C to measure how an organizational member
handles her or his conflict with superiors, subordinates, and peers, respectively"
(p. 123). So the conflicts are workplace and **power-asymmetric by design** in Forms A
and B, and peer-level in Form C.

**Actors versus observers:** the Discussion notes samples 1–4 "contained data from
actors, but Sample 5 had data from observers" (p. 130).

**Derived here or imported?** **Imported and tested.** The five-style scheme and the
28-item instrument come from Rahim (1983); the item-selection history (six successive
factor analyses, ~105 items considered, final 28 selected from a national sample of 1,219
managers) is summarised at p. 123. This paper contributes the confirmatory test with
LISREL 7 (Abstract, p. 122).

## 4. Frequency / base rates

**[none reported]** — no distribution of how often each style is used is given. The paper
reports factor loadings, fit indices and invariance tests, not prevalence. Its data are
Likert ratings, which produce mean scores per subscale rather than counts of observed
behaviour.

## 5. Reliability

**Inter-rater reliability: [none reported]** — and it is not the relevant statistic here,
because ROCI-II is a self-report questionnaire rather than a coding scheme applied by
raters.

What the paper does report (p. 123, summarising Rahim 1983):
- **test–retest reliability .60–.83** (collegiate sample, n = 119, one-week interval,
  p < .0001);
- **Cronbach's α .72–.76** for managerial and **.65–.80** for collegiate samples.
The paper compares these favourably with the Blake–Mouton, Hall, Lawrence–Lorsch and
Thomas–Kilmann instruments (p. 123).

## 6. Operationalisation — how a category gets assigned

**This is the critical caveat for the FYP: a category is assigned from a person's
self-ratings, not from anything observable in an interaction.**

- The respondent rates **28 statements** about their own behaviour on a **5-point Likert
  scale** (5 = strongly agree, 1 = strongly disagree); "[a] higher score represents
  greater use of a conflict style" (p. 123).
- Items are split **seven, six, five, six, and four** across the five styles respectively
  (p. 123).
- Assignment is therefore **continuous and per-person** — each respondent receives five
  subscale scores, not one label — and it is **retrospective self-description**, not
  behaviour coded from a transcript.
- The paper provides **no rules for assigning a style to an utterance or to an observed
  episode**; no coding manual, no worked examples of coded behaviour `[not stated in
  paper]`.

## 7. Limitations stated by the authors

- **Full invariance did not hold.** The abstract claims support for invariance across
  referent roles, organisational levels and "4 of the 5 samples" (p. 122); the Discussion
  states "the factor loadings were not invariant across the five samples" and reports that
  dropping Sample 5 (observers) yielded non-significant differences for samples 1–4
  (p. 130).
- **Replication needed**: "In future studies, models developed in the present study should
  be tested with data from other samples" (§Discussion, p. 130).
- The paper also records, without endorsing, the standing challenge to the five-style
  paradigm from Weider-Hatfield (1988) and Hocker and Wilmot (1991) (p. 123).

## 8. Relevance to this FYP

- **It supplies the five-style vocabulary with authoritative definitions** (p. 123), which
  is what the project would need if it labels an agent's overall stance in an exchange.
- **It does not supply a way to apply those labels to talk.** Everything here is
  self-report (§6 above). Using the five styles to code an LLM transcript is an extension
  of the instrument beyond its validated use. [inference — the paper makes no claim about
  transcript coding either way.]
- **It documents that the taxonomy's dimensionality is contested** (p. 123, p. 130), so
  the project cannot present "five styles" as settled fact.
- **Power asymmetry is baked into the instrument's forms** (p. 123). The FYP's two hallway
  agents are peers with no institutional relationship, closest to Form C. [inference]
